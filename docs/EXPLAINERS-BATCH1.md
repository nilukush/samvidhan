# Explainer Batch 1: Part III and Article 51A

Human review gate for Step 10. Each explainer below sits on its article page today. Read against the article text; flag any inaccuracy. Verified against the official edition as on 1 May 2026.

## Article 12. Definition

This article defines who counts as the State for this Part. The definition covers the Government and Parliament of India, state governments and legislatures, and local or other bodies under government control or within India. The rights in this Part bind all of these bodies.

## Article 13. Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of the fundamental rights

This article makes fundamental rights stronger than ordinary law. Any law, old or new, that conflicts with these rights stops working to that extent. The word law covers ordinances, rules, orders, and customs. Governments cannot bypass rights by choosing a different legal form.

## Article 14. Equality before law

This article says every person is equal before the law and gets equal protection of the laws. The State cannot treat one person better or worse than another in like cases. Courts use it to test arbitrary state action, because unequal treatment without a fair reason usually fails here.

## Article 15. Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth

This article bans discrimination by the State against citizens only on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. No citizen can be barred from shops, restaurants, hotels, wells, tanks, or public places on those grounds. The State may still make special provisions for women, children, and disadvantaged classes, and for seats in education and jobs.

## Article 16. Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment

This article promises an equal chance for all citizens to get public jobs. No citizen can be kept out of a government job only for reasons of religion, race, caste, sex, birth, family line, or residence. Parliament or a state legislature may set residence rules for some posts. The State may also reserve posts for backward classes.

## Article 17. Abolition of Untouchability

This article abolishes untouchability. Its practice in any form is forbidden. So is enforcing any disability that arises from it. Parliament backed this with a criminal law against untouchability. No custom, and no payment, can justify treating any person as untouchable.

## Article 18. Abolition of titles

This article stops the State from giving or recognising titles of nobility, and no citizen may accept one from a foreign state. Awards for military or academic distinction are allowed. They cannot be used as titles attached to names. The aim is to keep citizens equal, without ranks the government can hand out.

## Article 19. Protection of certain rights regarding freedom of speech, etc

This article gives citizens six freedoms: speech and expression; assembly without arms; forming associations; movement; residence; and any profession or trade. Each freedom can face reasonable limits. The State may set limits for stated public reasons, such as security, public order, or the rights of others. Only citizens hold these freedoms.

## Article 20. Protection in respect of conviction for offences

This article protects people in criminal cases in three ways. A person cannot be punished under a law that was not in force when the act was done. A person cannot be tried and punished twice for the same offence. And a person cannot be forced to testify against himself. These protections apply to everyone, citizen or not.

## Article 21. Protection of life and personal liberty

This article says no person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except by a fair legal procedure. Courts have read it broadly. The right to life means a life with dignity, and it covers things like a clean environment, health, shelter, and privacy. It is the most used fundamental right in Indian courts.

## Article 22. Protection against arrest and detention in certain cases

This article protects people who are arrested. An arrested person must be told the reason. The person may consult a lawyer of choice. The person must be produced before a magistrate within twenty four hours. Preventive detention is allowed, but only under a fair procedure with advisory boards and time limits set by law.

## Article 23. Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour

This article bans traffic in human beings and forced labour in any form. No one can be made to work without pay, and bonded labour is illegal. The State may require compulsory service for a public purpose, such as during an emergency. It must not impose that service by religion, caste, or class.

## Article 24. Prohibition of employment of children in factories, etc

This article bans employing children below fourteen in factories, mines, or other risky work. It protects children from labour that harms health and safety. It works with the right to education in Article 21A, so children study instead of doing dangerous work.

## Article 25. Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religion

This article guarantees freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise, and spread religion, for all persons. The State may regulate religious activity for public order, morality, and health. It may also open Hindu religious institutions to all classes. Wearing and carrying kirpans counts as part of the Sikh religion.

## Article 26. Freedom to manage religious affairs

This article lets every religious group manage its own affairs. A group may set up institutions, own property, and run them by its own faith. This holds subject to public order, morality, and health. The State may still pass laws for social reform, or for opening institutions to all classes.

## Article 27. Freedom as to payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion

This article stops the State from forcing any person to pay taxes whose money is used to promote one religion. Public money collected from all cannot be spent on the institutions of a single faith. This keeps the state out of religious promotion.

## Article 28. Freedom as to attendance at religious instruction or religious worship in certain educational institutions

This article keeps religious teaching out of institutions maintained fully by government funds. Institutions that receive aid may give religious teaching, but only with consent. No student can be made to attend religious teaching or worship without the consent of a guardian.

## Article 29. Protection of interests of minorities

This article protects the culture and language of any section of citizens, including minorities. Any citizen has the right to conserve a distinct language, script, or culture. No citizen can be denied entry to a State run institution only for reasons of religion, race, caste, or language.

## Article 30. Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions

This article gives religious and language minorities the right to start and run their own schools and colleges. The State cannot be unfair to such institutions when giving aid. Laws that control them must respect the minority's choice of what is taught and who teaches.

## Article 31. Compulsory acquisition of property

This article was omitted by the Constitution Forty fourth Amendment Act in 1978. It earlier made the right to property a fundamental right. Property is now only an ordinary legal right under Article 300A. The State may take property for public use with a valid law and a fair process.

## Article 32. Remedies for enforcement of rights conferred by this Part

This article is the right to enforce every other right in this Part. A person whose rights are broken may go straight to the Supreme Court. The Court can pass directions and writs such as habeas corpus and certiorari. This remedy is itself a fundamental right. It cannot be suspended except as the Constitution allows during an emergency.

## Article 33. Power of Parliament to modify the rights conferred by this Part in their application to Forces, etc

This article lets Parliament limit or end the fundamental rights of members of the armed forces, police, and intelligence services. The limits exist so that they keep discipline and do their duties properly. This is why service rules can curb speech and association rights for those in the forces.

## Article 34. Restriction on rights conferred by this Part while martial law is in force in any area

This article protects persons acting under martial law from suits for acts done in that role. It also lets Parliament shield officers and servants of the Union for acts done to keep or restore order in areas where martial law holds.

## Article 35. Legislation to give effect to the provisions of this Part

This article gives Parliament alone the power to make laws for enforcing the rights in this Part. That includes the power to fix punishment for acts the Constitution declares to be crimes. State legislatures cannot make such laws. This keeps the sanctions for rights uniform across the country.

## Article 21A. Right to education

This article makes free and compulsory education a right for every child aged six to fourteen. The State must provide this education. A child can claim it as a matter of right, not charity. It was added in 2002 so that schooling becomes a duty of the State.

## Article 31A. Saving of laws providing for acquisition of estates, etc

This article saves certain laws from being struck down for violating fundamental rights. It covers laws about acquiring estates and similar property reforms. Land reform laws cannot be attacked merely for limiting property rights. Courts later read this saving as subject to the basic structure of the Constitution.

## Article 31B. Validation of certain Acts and Regulations

This article protects laws placed in the Ninth Schedule from challenge under fundamental rights. Laws added to that schedule are shielded even if they conflict with Part III. In 2007 the Supreme Court held that laws added after 1973 can still be tested against the basic structure of the Constitution.

## Article 31C. Saving of laws giving effect to certain directive principles

This article shields laws that carry out certain directive principles. Those principles deal with sharing resources and reducing wealth gaps. Such laws cannot be struck down under Articles 14 and 19. Since 1973, the shield does not cover laws that damage the basic structure of the Constitution.

## Article 31D. Saving of laws in respect of anti-national activities

This article was inserted by the Forty second Amendment to save laws against anti national activities. It never came into effect. It was omitted by the Constitution Forty third Amendment Act in 1977. The Constitution today contains no such article.

## Article 32A. Constitutional validity of State laws not to be considered in proceedings under article 32

This article was inserted by the Forty second Amendment and omitted by the Forty third Amendment in 1977. It had removed the power of courts to examine State laws in certain proceedings under Article 32. It never took effect and no longer exists.

## Article 51A. Fundamental duties

This article lists the duties of every citizen. Respect the Constitution, the national flag, and the anthem. Cherish the ideals of the freedom struggle. Protect the unity of India and its environment. Develop scientific temper and humanism. Protect public property and strive for excellence. Courts cannot punish a breach directly, but laws may be read in its light.
